Who I am and how I work
BK21 Fellow UIAI Lab · Ulsan

AI Researcher | Machine Learning Engineer | Advance Signal Processing

// Prognostics & Health Management (PHM)

I didn't start out chasing bearings, pipelines, and milling cutters — I started out curious about why machines fail quietly before they fail loudly, and whether the right signal, read the right way, could give an engineer enough warning to act. That question is still what gets me into the lab every morning.

Today I'm a combined MS–PhD student and BK21 research fellow in the Ulsan Industrial Artificial Intelligence (UIAI) Lab at the University of Ulsan, South Korea, working under Prof. Jong-Myon Kim. I earned my bachelor's in Computer Systems Engineering from UET Peshawar in 2023, and since joining UIAI in March 2024 I've spent my time listening to machines — literally. I work with acoustic emission signals, vibration data, and time-frequency representations to catch degradation early, model how it evolves, and predict how much useful life a component has left.

What I enjoy most is the meeting point between classical signal processing — FFT, STFT, wavelets, Koopman and spectral methods — and modern deep learning, including Vision Transformers, conformal prediction, and domain adaptation. I care less about chasing the flashiest architecture and more about building models that an engineer on a factory floor could actually trust. That mindset has led to 25+ peer-reviewed papers in venues like IEEE Access, Scientific Reports, Engineering Failure Analysis, and MDPI Sensors, plus two Best Paper Awards, including one at FICTA 2025 in the UK and one in ICRAI 2026 Pakistan.

I'm now looking for my next challenge: a postdoctoral position, industry role, or research collaboration — in Saudi Arabia, Europe, or elsewhere — where I can push trustworthy, deployable prognostics for safety-critical industrial systems out of the lab and into the real world.

— Muhammad Umar, Ulsan, South Korea

Focus areas

Research interests

Where curiosity meets industrial impact

Intelligent systems that don't need perfect data

Robust, data-efficient, generalizable deep learning for industrial and healthcare applications — built to work in the messy conditions of the real world, not just the lab.

Listening to machines in every domain

Physics-aware representation learning with FFT, STFT, wavelets and adaptive transforms — turning raw vibration and acoustic signals into machinery-health intelligence.

Catching failure before it happens. Interpretable, domain-adaptive approaches for early fault detection, degradation modeling and predictive maintenance across critical machinery.

AI an engineer can actually trust

Transparency, reliability assessment, uncertainty quantification and human-centric explainability for AI deployed in safety-critical environments.

Beyond CNNs: the next generation of models

Vision transformers, multimodal models and generative approaches applied to industrial inspection and biomedical imaging.

Teaching models the physics they're missing

Integrating domain knowledge, physical constraints and graph neural networks for smarter sensing in complex, interconnected systems.

Learning more from less

Self-supervised, few-shot and cross-modal learning that keeps monitoring systems scalable even when labeled data is scarce.

Intelligence that fits on the factory floor

Lightweight models, on-device intelligence and efficient deployment strategies built for real industrial IoT constraints.

A living model of every machine

AI-enabled digital twins for continuous health awareness, reliability optimization and lifecycle management at scale.
